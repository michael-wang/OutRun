# v0.6 — Scenic Journey

**Owner priority:** “Next should improve scenery on both sides of the road and add more track variation.” Also fix wheels visibly not spinning during big drifts; bring the open-top car's driver/occupants to life, notably exaggerated female long-hair wind motion.

**Base:** EXISTING PLAYABLE LOCAL v0.5 (`645b7ed` implementation, `9a05fc3` handoff; verify current local HEAD).
**Milestone intent:** Make continued driving rewarding because the world and the driving *rhythm* change. Retain the first human-approved satisfying arcade driving experience. Don't rebuild it.

## P0-A — Wheel animation credibility hotfix

The new convertible in v0.5 can display extreme 50–60° rendered body yaw, exposing that its wheels appear static/frozen. Fix this before building new landscapes.

- Inspect existing procedural/model wheel hierarchy and update loop. Determine whether rotation is missing, applied around a wrong local axle, covered by stationary caps, or visually aliased at high RPM.
- Each wheel should actually roll about its axle based on **real longitudinal/world travel distance per update and effective tire radius**, rather than HUD speed alone. It should stop when the car is truly stopped and respond when accelerating/braking; use delta-time-safe math and avoid invisible rotation due to near-exact frame-period sampling.
- Front wheels should exhibit **plausible bounded steering angle** responding to applied steering, nested separately from wheel rolling. When the body performs a 60° cinematic yaw, don't simply rotate the front wheels 60° around a wrong axis or detach them from the vehicle.
- Make rotation *visually apparent at high speed*: visible wheel spokes/tread/trim or carefully stylized rotating/blurred spokes where needed, without unpleasant flicker, model clipping, detached wheels or fake high-speed spinning when stopped.
- Test both right and left strong drift, ordinary coast, launch/stop and brake; record front/rear wheel angle/spin telemetry plus actual close-view motion footage. Do not mark PASS only because numerical rotation advances while footage looks frozen.

## P0-B — THREE scenic acts on one flowing road

Build one continuous drive that changes to **three distinctly identifiable environments**:

| Scenic act | Identity and near/mid/far layers | Signature road experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Azure Coast** | Deep blue sea, coastal cliffs, pale houses, palms, water horizon; near road posts/railings, one recognizable coastal landmark | Two fast visible sweeps around coastline and a climb/crest; enjoy top speed |
| **Golden Canyon** | Ochre/red stone cuts, sparse dry trees/shrubs, layered mesas/cliffs, distinctive rock formations; roadside edge walls/posts | Rising S-curves, narrow-looking but fair technical/braking corner, strong elevation changes |
| **Sunlit Coastal City** | Colorful coastal midrises, distinct rooflines/window rhythms, promenade/light poles, urban trees and far skyline/harbor motif | Broad boulevard sweep, downhill/urban bends, short breathing acceleration before next act |

The table specifies design intent, not mandatory exact geographical geography. Keep an **original clean stylized sunlit palette**. Distinctive materials, prop SILHOUETTES, geometry and near/mid/far depth must communicate each scene. No generic cyberpunk/neon night, not flat low-poly placeholders or repeating identical cube buildings.

### Scenic delivery minimums

- **Two clearly perceived transitions** (Coast→Canyon→City) during a normal roughly **100–150s** fast-play journey. Target each scenic act approximately **30–45s at a documented representative fast 1.75× world pace**. The stage schedule is **travel-distance-based**; slower players spend longer in each.
- Two or more recognizable visual landmark moments per act are desirable, but at least ONE unmistakable large landmark per act is required, plus changing roadside prop patterns along the act. A screenshot with stage labels hidden should make all three acts easy to distinguish.
- Stages have their own foreground/midground/background, density and rhythm: do not pass by simply recoloring the sky or alternating palm colors. Near objects remain close enough for strong optical flow; distant silhouettes move slowly.
- Don't instantly teleport, flicker, reload or visibly pop scenery at act boundaries. Blend prop families/material/fog/ground over a transition stretch **defined in distance**, ideally hidden by an authored bend/crest or gradual overlap. No big paused loading screen; no runtime network requests.
- Looping course must hide or smoothly bridge City→Coast as well if looped. If only a one-way scenic tour is implemented, returning from the end should use a clear, intentional restart transition rather than an obvious mid-drive visual snap; the game must remain replayable.
- Keep an explicit small **Scene Act / Track Segment data table** in code: act ID, distance bounds, scene palette/prop families, near/far landmark locations and road beat labels. Reuse existing generation infrastructure. **Don't implement a gigantic procedural editor or biome system.**
- Deterministic scenery generation and precomputed/cached geometry (or small visible-range chunk streaming) should minimize allocations during driving. Reuse prop meshes/materials where reasonable, use instancing for repeated objects and lower detail at distance, with low shadow cost on Pixel 8.
- Preserve original assets/licensing; record source+license for any external models/textures in `docs/ASSETS.md`. If no external assets, say so. No hotlinked runtime content.

### Transition tests

Use development-only deterministic start/query flags such as `?stage=coast`, `?stage=canyon`, `?stage=city` (or equivalent) for stage-specific QA. They must not activate autopilot or change normal driving controls. Record actual 20–30s browser clips spanning **both** Coast→Canyon and Canyon→City, showing uninterrupted coherent road, continuous forward flow and no sudden FPS stalls.

## P0-C — Real road and scenic variety remain GAMEPLAY-FIRST

More scenic acts must not reduce the road to a long dull straight, nor invalidate our calibrated required-steering system.

- Keep **Arcade Lateral Balance** and inverse-track authoring: design steering demand, allowed speed, segment distance, grade/width first; derive actual curvature and 3D road geometry. Scene act only selects art/environment parameters, never secretly changes steering pressure or traction mid-bend.
- Retain existing calibrated **Low, Medium, High, S, Extreme braking-required** challenges and full-gas navigable long LEFT/RIGHT sweepers. Distribute challenges meaningfully across the three acts; avoid 30–45 seconds of identical straight road just to showcase new background art.
- Make track rhythm **recognizably different** per act: coast includes a fast wide sweeper and sea-view crest; canyon has an S-series plus a tight yet readable brake-required corner; city has a wide boulevard curve and descending faster section. At least **two clearly visible climb/crest/descent** features over the route, one near a themed landmark. These must be actual 3D road elevations, not mountain skybox alone.
- Preserve ability to maintain near-top speed through sweeping bends, with separate Extreme corners that require braking **because of calibrated curvature**. No hidden game difficulty changes simply due to a new visual biome.
- Keep forgiving bounded shoulder/visible road-edge, off-road slowdown, car/world positioning and good lookahead. Road/terrain must not intersect buildings/cliffs or vanish under mountains; avoid abrupt spline/mesh/camera seams.
- Document a small track beat table with intended required-steer, target speed, distance/elevation/act and actual observed time/distance under 1.75×. User should experience challenge variety alongside scenery variety, not a road prettification alone.

## P1 — Lightweight expressive occupants (only after P0)

The convertible already has a driver and another occupant. The owner requests **driver animation and exaggerated female hair flutter** during fast driving. Keep the same car assets unless fixing wheels requires small structural changes.

- **Driver:** simple steering/arm/shoulder/head movement synchronized with wheel input and forceful bends; subtle acceleration/braking response. No full skeletal rig needed; use small procedural pivot groups or existing animation clips if readily available.
- **Long-haired female occupant** (driver or passenger based on the actual established car design): create/animate visible hair locks/strands or stylized clumps whipping BACK at speed. Increase motion from stop→cruise→fast, add periodic gust flutter and cornering-side lean; avoid hard mechanical loops, hair detaching or poking through seat/head/body.
- Two visible people should react modestly and coherently to high-speed drift, e.g. torso/head lean, not both frozen. These are playful *visual* responses; do NOT change driving dynamics.
- Hair needs to be readable in normal Pixel 8 landscape chase view, not only in a microscopic close-up. Include one short clip of straight high speed and one large corner.
- Keep fps cost trivial; no heavy cloth physics or large rig. If core scenic stages/performance not sound, clearly **DEFER** occupant animations rather than compromising P0.

## Performance, invariants and non-goals

Preserve:
- 1.75× accepted world pace; inverse Required Steering→curvature design; 4 soft detents/side with stable touch, 50–60° peak rendered body yaw; no residual physics yaw or lane auto-centering.
- Pixel 8 20:9 Android Chrome landscape fullscreen/PWA, two-thumb horizontal steering strip + vertical gas/brake, handedness swap, long-press prevention, safe areas, and no sticky controls; PC A/D steer + Space gas + Enter brake.
- Fixed chase FOV/dolly, 20–25% car frame occupancy, left-of-car animated analog needle km/h, audio/pause, playable off-road slowdown/bounded recovery.
- Local browser app, Three.js + TypeScript + Vite. No new game engine, backend, accounts, traffic/opponents, collision rivals, difficulty director, route forks, scoring, car selection, PWA rewrite or complex new content tooling.

On desktop, compare frame time/draw call/memory across stage boundaries; avoid visible pop, CPU/GC spikes or excessive aliasing. Aim smooth sustained play on actual Pixel 8 (≥30 FPS targeted; 60 preferred), but do not invent real-device metrics from desktop emulation.
