# v0.1 — First Drive: implementation specification

**Milestone:** First locally playable autonomous Codex delivery  
**Status:** Ready for implementation  
**Priority:** A compelling driving feel and a good-looking scene beat a broad feature list.

## 1. User experience and flow

1. Open a local browser URL.
2. See a title/menu with **Drive**, **Settings**, and **Sound** (simple is fine).
3. Start the game immediately on a **sunny coastal road** in third-person chase view.
4. Hold accelerator, steer through winding corners, optionally brake, experience drifting and tire squeal.
5. Continue driving for **at least 60 seconds of forward travel** without a missing track/dead end; looping track or seamless recycled sections are acceptable.
6. Pause/resume and restart without reloading the browser. No ending, scoring, opponents, or progression required.

Aim for an evocative ~60–90 second sequence at ordinary enthusiastic speed, not a measured race lap.

## 2. Rendering and environment

**Required**
- Three.js rendering with a **real 3D car and drivable 3D road**; no sprite-only pseudo-3D highway.
- Road has at least one clear **left bend**, one clear **right bend**, and one **crest/dip**; the driving surface and vehicle positions agree visually throughout.
- Road has visible lanes/edge lines, shoulder, readable boundaries/guardrail or terrain, and stable mesh continuity. Vehicle must not disappear through the road or clip excessively.
- Scenic Mediterranean coast: sky and clouds, blue sea, distant landforms, close roadside detail (e.g., palms, coastal cliffs, vegetation, small buildings), appropriate light/shadows/fog for depth.
- One appealing original stylized 3D sports car with readable silhouette, wheels, paint, and lights/detail. A well-chosen redistributable model is acceptable with documented license.
- Smooth chase camera with enough look-ahead to see an approaching curve. Respect mobile safe areas.

**Acceptable shortcuts**
- Far scenery can use sprites/billboards/sky textures; low-cost instancing and simple meshes are encouraged.
- A designed procedural stylized car can replace an external model if assets are blocked, but a bare rectangular box on wheels **does not** meet visual quality.
- Dynamic resolution/shadow simplification on mobile is acceptable and desirable.
- No full vehicle simulation, damage model, weather system, AI traffic, or asset-pipeline tooling required.

## 3. Driving model

**Required**
- Steering, accelerator, and brake are **independent inputs**. Vehicle does not auto-accelerate.
- Acceleration from a stop and braking from speed are obvious, proportional, and smooth. Show current speed in the HUD (arcade speed units / km/h-style label is fine; do not imply exact physical calibration).
- Steering changes the car's position/heading relative to the road, not just a cosmetic body tilt. Vehicle follows the evolving curved terrain/road.
- At medium/high speed and significant steering/cornering demand, produce a **controllable drift/slip**: visible yaw/roll or tire-angle response, a skid sound, and optionally tire smoke. Slide starts and ends smoothly.
- Grip and speed behavior favor fun: slight steering assistance, recoverable drift, no frequent uncontrollable spins. Off-road movement should be visually handled and recoverable (e.g. slowed/limited with visual feedback).
- Camera remains smooth and forward-looking at low speed, high speed, and in curves; avoid excessive jitter.

**Implementation guidance, not a mandate:** a spline/centerline + swept road mesh and simple heading/lateral-velocity/speed variables can work; avoid full rigid-body tire physics. Tune controls by actual playtesting.

## 4. Controls

**Desktop**
- `A/D` or `←/→`: steer.
- `W` or `↑`: accelerate.
- `S` or `↓`: brake.
- `Escape` (or `P`): pause/resume; `R`: restart; sound mute available in UI.
- Multiple keys held at once must work (e.g., accelerate + steer + brake releases).

**Mobile (landscape primary target)**
- **Default:** left thumb: two large left/right steering buttons; right thumb: vertical accelerator **above** brake buttons.
- **Swapped:** right thumb gets steering; left thumb gets vertical accelerator/brake; change in Settings.
- Multi-touch must reliably handle simultaneous steering + accelerator and moving/releasing either finger.
- Use Pointer Events / touch-safe behavior, good button hit areas, visual pressed feedback, CSS safe areas, and prevent touch-scrolling during play.
- Store handedness setting locally and restore it on reload. Both layouts keep accelerator above brake.
- If opened portrait, show a friendly rotate-to-landscape hint rather than rendering broken controls.

## 5. Sound and interface

- Engine sound responds at least roughly to throttle or speed. Tire squeal is clearly distinct, starts during actual slip and stops when grip returns.
- Browser autoplay rules: audio begins only after user interaction. Provide mute toggle; no third-party music or game samples copied from SEGA.
- Minimal HUD: speed, pause/reset, sound; controls must not block most of the road view.
- Menu and settings should be simple, responsive, and visually consistent with the bright coastal theme. The prototype may use project codename in developer UI, but don't create branded promotional assets under that name.

## 6. Tech, stability, and performance

- Vite + TypeScript + Three.js. Minimal dependencies. Make real npm scripts for `dev`, `build`, `test`, and `test:e2e`.
- Client-side only, locally runnable without keys, paid services, backend, or runtime network asset requests.
- Responsive canvas at desktop 1280×720 and landscape-mobile ~844×390; no horizontal overflow or unreadable essential controls.
- Graceful handling of resize, tab blur/pause, input release, and audio lifecycle.
- Aim for responsive frame pacing on a normal desktop and mid-range phone; do not invent FPS measurements. Automatically adjust pixel ratio/shadows if needed.
- Use explicit inspectable state/test hooks or deterministic test mode when necessary to automate speed/steering/braking and mobile layout assertions without weakening gameplay.

## 7. Not in v0.1

- Opponent/traffic AI, collision racing, checkpoints, finish line, leaderboards, store, economy, garage, car customization, multiple tracks, route selection, online features, accounts, soundtrack composition.
- Full-scale component frameworks, ECS, physics-engine architecture, complex art-asset build systems, fake test dashboards, or extensive procedural-generation pipelines.

## 8. Priority order under time pressure

1. Boots locally; **car and continuous 3D winding road are driveable**.
2. Real accelerator/brake/steer, stable camera, meaningful cornering/drift.
3. Coastal visual quality, strong speed cues, skid sound.
4. Mobile two-thumb input and side swap.
5. Automated regression/browser checks, screenshots, cleanup, and handoff.

Do not quietly drop #4 or tests; if time/tools run out, report incomplete items honestly and provide the best working slice.
