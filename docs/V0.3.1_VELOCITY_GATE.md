# v0.3.1 — Velocity Gate: prove that high speed looks fast

**Decision:** This is a focused **go/no-go prototype milestone**, not a general v0.4 feature build. The user will decide whether the game is worth continuing based primarily on **perceived speed**.

**Base:** Existing playable v0.3 local branch, implementation `97de506` + handoff `40169f8` (inspect actual HEAD; preserve any later changes).

## Evidence and diagnosis

User's v0.3 gameplay recording (~61s, desktop capture) shows the displayed speed reaching **234 km/h around 6s** and staying near it for much of the drive, yet the road and scenery feel slow. The car framing is noticeably better than v0.2; **do not erase that progress**.

Primary suspects to investigate in actual source, not assume:
- HUD km/h / game speed scalar may not map to sensible forward **world displacement per real second**.
- Track/car/scenery may use inconsistent world scales: road excessively wide, landmarks too distant or too widely spaced.
- Chase camera smoothing, road segment recycling, spline coordinate updates, fixed-step accumulation or clipping may damp / incorrectly represent speed.
- Sparse lane dashes, rails and near-road landmarks provide insufficient optical-flow signals; remote mountains/sea alone don't communicate speed.
- Actual frame time or inconsistent dt logic may reduce motion.

Three.js has no inherent upper speed limit. Do not blame the 3D renderer before measuring the model and scene.

## P0-A: Instrument actual physical/game movement

Add small development-only telemetry, inspectable in browser / Playwright and optional toggled overlay:
- displayed speed, game movement scalar and (if defined) world-units-to-meters mapping;
- time-integrated actual **forward world distance per elapsed second**, and distance in **road widths per second** (world-unit independent);
- track spline progress delta, car world position/heading vs camera translation;
- road total width and distance between repeatable roadside features; marker pass events / sec;
- FPS and frame delta; note if low performance/lag changes the simulated distance per wall-clock second.

Audit all speed caps, easing, dt caps, unit conversion, road length/progress/streaming and camera update order. A game unit need not be a meter, but document a consistent scale. For reference, **234 km/h ≈65 m/s**, so if 1 unit = 1 m the world displacement should be ~65 units per wall-clock second, not merely '234' on the HUD.

Provide a small, reproducible **Speed Audit** in the handoff: data at low, mid and high stabilized actual game speeds, world-distance/s, widths/s, landmark pass cadence and FPS.

## P0-B: Make optical flow genuinely compelling

Tune the actual *driving* world-travel pipeline coherently. Road collision, spline sampling, off-road effect, steering and camera should all agree after any unit/velocity scaling.

- Include near-road reference elements regularly: poles, reflectors, low guardrail posts, readable road-edge paint and lane-dash sequences. Use simple instanced/merged geometry where appropriate; avoid tens of thousands of expensive meshes.
- Make near posts and road stripes cross the near field rapidly at high speed; distant mountains remain relatively stationary, producing **depth-dependent parallax**.
- If scene is over-scaled, adjust road/player/prop dimensions and spacing together rather than only increasing the displayed number or shrinking the car.
- If speed controller, delta-time or spline progress is artificially limited, fix that bottleneck and preserve frame-rate-independent motion.
- Make engine pitch/wind build with actual car speed/throttle and skid squeal stay linked to genuine drift; don't rely on sound alone.
- Maintain fixed camera FOV and speed-independent chase dolly; don't add aggressive screen shake, motion blur or fake zoom.

**Calibration aid:** Add a clearly developer-only speed/optical-flow comparison toggle or reproducible launch flag to drive the same straight marked segment at distinct settled speeds (e.g. low ~80 vs high ~230 shown units), with identical camera and same starting spatial conditions. An optional tunable world-forward-speed factor is okay during diagnosis, **but never just multiply HUD text, shift scenery independently of vehicle physics or silently change speed after evidence capture**. If a tunable factor is retained, keep it dev-only and label the tested value.

## Required moving proof

- Capture short genuine browser **video clips** at low and high speed over a comparable marked stretch of road with the **same camera**. Video timestamps must be clear; screenshot pairs alone are insufficient.
- Capture one continuous launch → acceleration → stable high-speed drive clip.
- Record marker pass intervals/counts or another objective near-field optical-flow proxy with underlying world-distance telemetry. The high-speed clip should show an unmistakable increase in roadside and lane-line flow relative to low speed.
- Actually inspect the clips. If motion still looks relaxed, tune and repeat instead of presenting green test counts.
- For review on Pixel 8, provide a playable local build and a quick A/B speed calibration switch if it meaningfully speeds the human decision. No need to build a permanent settings feature.

## Explicit non-goals

**Do NOT implement yet:** more powerful drift/turn animations, road rhythm overhaul, a convertible/driver animations, biomes, UI overhauls, rival cars, races and score systems. These requests are recorded in `docs/ARCADE_DESIGN_BACKLOG.md`. The user wants speed approved **first**.

Existing v0.3 framing (~24% reported), Pixel 8 two-thumb controls/fullscreen/PWA, long-press suppression, car and track, bounded off-road/penalty, recoverable steering, audio/pause and browser behavior must not regress.

## Handoff / stop condition

Create `docs/V0.3.1_HANDOFF.md` with:
1. root cause(s) identified **from inspected code**; include exact speed/scene units, caps and dt treatment;
2. before/after world-distance-per-second and near-marker pass cadence, plus camera FOV/distance;
3. desktop/landscape mobile screenshots and actual low/high/acceleration video clips;
4. build/unit/E2E results; actual FPS/device status;
5. outstanding limitations and an **explicit HUMAN GO/NO-GO decision** for perceived speed.

Stop after this proof-of-speed milestone. Do not assume a mathematical PASS proves the game is exciting: the user's firsthand Pixel 8 review is the decisive test.
