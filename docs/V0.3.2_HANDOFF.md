## What changed and why

v0.3.1's ~64.92 m/s was mathematically consistent but failed the user's speed-feeling gate. This experiment increases actual vehicle motion. It adds no scenery, camera tricks or unrelated features.

`src/pace.ts` selects the four allowed values, with 1.75 as the application default. Query selection and comparison controls are development-only; the production prototype uses the fixed 1.75 default. Invalid query values fall back to that default.

For indicated speed `v * 3.6`, let `t = clamp((v * 3.6 - 50) / 150, 0, 1)`. The effective multiplier is `1 + (selectedPace - 1) * t² * (3 - 2t)`. It remains 1 below 50 indicated km/h and reaches the selected value at 200. At 80, a selected 2× gives only 1.104×; at 150 it gives 1.74074×.

`src/handling.ts` multiplies **both** lateral and longitudinal velocity by this factor before the existing Frenet integration and corridor constraint. Heading/reference-frame updates consume that same longitudinal distance. The renderer samples the resulting car position on the unchanged spline, and the camera follows that real position. Scenery remains stationary. Road width is still 14 world metres; geometry scale, track, car and 12 m posts / 6 m dash periods are unchanged.

Active player yaw authority, steering-input response and tire response scale with the same factor to retain useful turning response per metre traveled. A first 2× keyboard test exposed steering-input lag; scaling that response too resolved the test. This is not a curvature-derived steering term: zero steering from a neutral state still leaves world heading unchanged and the car departs both meaningful bends. 1× multiplies by exactly one and reproduces the old handling. Ordinary acceleration/brake values remain unchanged in indicated-speed units; off-road drag reduces speed and therefore also reduces its amplification. Boundary-only contact still suppresses outward yaw, clamps lateral position and requires deliberate inward steering to recover.

Audio receives the effective travel-speed magnitude, preserving throttle and slip inputs. The new default reaches stronger engine/wind feedback sooner. At a boundary the positional projection can reduce realized progress below this commanded magnitude; measured audit values distinguish that constraint. Audio listening quality is not certified.

The gauge is now explicitly an **arcade indicated km/h** value, not a claim that 234 indicated equals 65 real m/s at boosted pace. Development `__drive.snapshot()` exposes selected/effective pace and commanded travel speed. `__drive.audit()` records actual world-path distance, track progress, camera movement, lateral change, heading, FPS and marker crossings per wall-clock window. The final development overlay displays the measured last one-second world rate; it shows no result until a window exists. Recordings preceded that readout-label refinement and display the nominal effective velocity at the top; all quantitative results below use measured world displacement from the raw audit, not that label.

Camera remains unchanged: 76° horizontal FOV, 8 m rear and 2.6 m elevated offset, vertical FOV 38.7412° at 960×432 and 47.4484° at 1280×720. No speed-dependent FOV/dolly, shake, blur or independent background motion. The 0.25 s long-stall cap remains; normal rendering time is integrated in ≤1/120 s steps. Backgrounding pauses the game. No new assets or dependencies were added.

Along-track speed is slightly below full world-path speed because of curvature, lateral correction and grade. See `evidence/v0.3.2/pace-summary.json` and each `pace-*/{low,mid,high,launch}.json` for raw one-second windows, wall time, simulation time, heading/pose and inputs.

Real browser callback throttling at 2× measured approximately **129.83 m/s at 60 FPS** and **129.92 m/s at 30 FPS**, a difference under 0.1%. The test permits 5% to avoid machine-timing flakiness; deterministic 30/60 substep tests require position/lateral/heading agreement to six decimal places across all variants. PC recordings used Edge / ANGLE / NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti; high-speed 1× and 2× recordings both averaged ~60 FPS with per-window p95 frame intervals ~16.8 ms. These are **not physical Pixel FPS results**. Encoded WebM is 25 FPS and can underrepresent smoothness at the fastest pace.

If even 2× fails Michael's speed test, do not escalate arbitrarily to 3–4×. Measured motion is already ~130 m/s and 9.27 road widths/s.
