### 1. Upshift torque interruption, not fake speed subtraction

**Owner observation:** the six-gear shifting is audible but driving speed/acceleration does not seem to drop during an upshift; wants more physically plausible shift feel.

An automatic/DCT-style upshift normally manages output torque and engine RPM across a short shift interval. RPM **should drop**, but vehicle km/h need not drop every time; depending on drag, load, grade and remaining transmitted torque it may level off or dip slightly. **Implement a real transient in wheel-driving force / net longitudinal acceleration**, not a cosmetic forced deduction from HUD speed. The owner wants a perceptible mechanical pause under full throttle.

- Inspect local v0.7C.1 model; integrate an explicit upshift event phase e.g. torque-reduction→gear engagement/RPM sync→torque restoration. Initial short envelope ~100–300ms, tuned in browser to avoid weird gear jolts. Actual force/acceleration and road distance integration must agree.
- During the torque interruption, normal coast/drag (and incline where model explicitly supports it) can cause a small *natural* speed dip; on other speeds the car may simply stop accelerating briefly. Do NOT require a large negative jump, direct subtract-km/h operation or reset an RPM-based proxy to reduce road speed. Keep physical speed continuous.
- Ensure there is perceptible acceleration notch at all FIVE automatic upshifts during full-gas 0→300 and a coherent RPM drop at the gear changes. No duplicate shifts, shift hunting, turbo-like speed surge, oscillating torque or braking feel at 1→2. Downshift/brake state remains coherent.
- Preserve praised warmer engine sound and exact RPM-to-audio sync; use current tune as reference and adjust only to match a realistic transient. Preserve actual 300 km/h max, 1.75× travel pace and Arcade Lateral Balance. Measure 0→100/200/300 after change; do not silently ruin successful 21.46s original tune.
- Deterministic telemetry around each shift: timestamp, gear old/new, rpm before/after, torque/traction factor, world-speed value and finite-difference speed/acceleration at 30/60fps; compare pre- and post-tune traces. Ideally show a subtle non-negative-to-negative acceleration transient under full gas at representative high-gear shift when drag dominates, without faking a mandatory drop at every speed.

## Objective: fix the mistaken unit of composition

Owner rejected the previous v0.7C **16-route product design**, which had exactly **one Curvature C, one Elevation E and one Width W per ENTIRE L2**, causing 30–60s roads to feel like a single prolonged effect. Owner's “3–5 L1” was AN EXAMPLE, never a mandated count. Reference screenshots of a classic ~37-second drive display several separate *perceptual beats* and overlapping layers, but do not allow an exact ground-truth count of hidden L1 nodes.

**Correct new model:**
- **L1 = local event/profile INSTANCE**: a primitive family C/E/W, concrete params, `startDistance`, `endDistance`, plus transition blending info.
- **L2 = authored/time-varying ~25–40s driving MICRO-JOURNEY**: multiple L1 instances at varying, overlapping distance ranges, not just a global C/E/W triple.
- **L3 = scene and scenery/terrain intent**; world rules constrain assets/surfaces, react to actual road profiles, can shift local scenic phases and landmarks without becoming unrestricted L1 road geometry.

A long *continuous* bend can be one sensible complex C primitive. Don't split it artificially merely to reach numeric “density.” Compare perceived beat flow, not a target count of code objects.

## Reusable minimal source structure

Use/adapt simplest possible structure compatible with current v0.7C local code:

`type Placement = {
  primitiveFamilyId: 'C01' | ... | 'E06' | ... | 'W04';
  startS: number;
  endS: number;
  parameters: {...};
  transition: 'smooth' | ...;
};`

`type L2Recipe = {
  id: string;
  revision: number;
  seed: number;
  worldPhases: [...];
  placements: Placement[];
};`

Do not build new giant rule DSL/editor/pipeline. Existing L1 profile evaluators should be reused/adapted to sample LOCAL normalized progress `t=(s-startS)/(endS-startS)` and combine into ONE effective signed curvature, height and road half-width per world distance. Same-channel overlapping intervals must have an explicit rule (non-overlap + continuity interpolation, or deterministic priority/crossfade); no double-command turn.

At several *different times* in a 30s route, new C profiles must activate; E rises/falls on a different schedule; W may expand/narrow when meaningful. Drive can be flat or constant width for intervals. **Do not require a lane change simply to satisfy a matrix row.**
