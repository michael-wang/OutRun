# v0.4.2 — Corner Exit & Road Rhythm

**Source:** Human PC playtest of v0.4.1 (~32s video, 25 Oct 2026).
**Base:** Local playable v0.4.1 at `4d4b05c` implementation / `34b6a3e` handoff (inspect current HEAD).
**Decision:** Keep the **new analog steering input** and the accepted **1.75× arcade speed**. Fix the difficulty of recovering at **corner exit**, then replace overly long/easy straight stretches with intentionally designed bends and stronger elevation.

## Observed problem and hypothesis discipline

- Player says **entering turns became more fun** with v0.4.1's analog input.
- On exits, correcting the turn is hard: vehicle often drifts/carries across the roadway to the opposite side. Around ~2s and ~18s of the provided gameplay recording, the car approaches roadway boundaries. The video does not expose exact steering inputs; **do not claim a specific physics root cause without instrumenting the code**.
- Road has too many long near-straights and insufficient meaningful up/down profile.

Do **not** address this by merely lowering overall steering sensitivity, slowing down world pace, adding a centerline magnetic force, or deleting the drift feature.

## P0-A — Corner EXIT, not corner ENTRY

The driver's steering intention, car heading and velocity direction are different variables. Expose and measure all relevant ones.

1. Inspect v0.4.1 input mapping from `steeringTarget` [-1,1] through commanded yaw, filtered yaw rate, actual vehicle world heading, velocity heading, slip/lateral velocity, road-tangent heading and lateral offset. Determine why post-corner motion lingers: input-release easing? residual yaw velocity? high slip angle? overly weak countersteer? A mistaken projection update? Identify observed root cause in code.
2. **Preserve prompt turn-in**. Absolute touch left/center/right still maps to full continuous steering range; PC A/D still moves that same virtual position and springs quickly back on release. Do not go back to binary steering or heavy ramp-in.
3. On **neutral steering**, new commanded yaw should settle quickly, and any unwanted *transient* yaw rate and lateral slip should rapidly damp. The vehicle maintains its own current world heading and forward movement. Do **not** snap heading to road tangent, auto-center in a lane or automatically steer through the curve.
4. The player should be able to briefly **countersteer** and stabilize after a hard bend without the car coasting across an entire lane from stale steering/side slip. Reverse direction must react promptly; no long lag through cached steering state.
5. Keep substantial **intentional** arcade drift available under appropriate strong control, without requiring long realistic tire inertia. You may use a quickly damped arcade velocity/body attitude model, but the steering still affects actual path and off-road state. Visual body yaw alone must not conceal a wrong physical trajectory.
6. At accepted `1.75×` travel, test low (~80), medium (~150) and high (~230 displayed km/h) steering. The steering should feel forgiving but not auto-solve turns. Small fraction inputs give precise adjustment; full lock gives enough authority for a challenging but navigable bend. Right/left behavior symmetric.
7. Do not make "neutral = straight along the **road**": if vehicle heading was misaligned at release, the player still needs to correct. The design improvement is predictable and fast **vehicle response to input**, not automatic navigation.

### Replay examples / indicative calibration

Run deterministic tests and **actual rendered-browser** drives on a consistent curve:
- 150ms moderate left steering → release: quick response and prompt yaw/slip settling after release; mirror right. Measure yaw-rate decay and lateral velocity separately.
- 800ms strong steer through a curve → reduce steer → short opposite countersteer → neutral. A reasonable player command sequence should stabilize the trajectory on/near pavement without a delayed swing to the far opposite shoulder; mirror directions.
- Continuous phone pointer travel 20%→50%→80% across the steering strip changes steer immediately; no unintended extra heading built up after returning midpoint.
- Default 1.75× versus dev 1×: compare control authority/exit stability to ensure pace tuning does not create an inertial bug.
- **Hands off** on a significant bend must still leave asphalt and receive the existing off-road slowdown; no invisible lane-keeping.

Starting tuning suggestion **not a binding physical law**: uncommanded yaw-rate should die down within roughly 0.15–0.3s after release; transient slip can decay over roughly 0.2–0.45s, depending on speed/drift state. The *car's heading angle itself* must not auto-return to the road tangent. If this heuristic is wrong, use data and playtest to choose better values.

The user must not need complicated hand-over-hand physics simulation to exit each bend; the intended loop is **turn in → choose your line → reduce/reverse steering → car responds promptly → accelerate**.

## P1 — Arcade rhythm: more bends, more hills

Do not simply increase turn sensitivity to create challenge. **Change the actual road geometry** while preserving reachability and the strong 1.75× speed experience.

### Suggested repeating dramatic beat pattern

At a representative high but realistic gameplay pace, **translate durations into track-distance segments**; never trigger the bends at fixed elapsed time:

| Experience | Indicative duration | Road character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening acceleration | 6–7s | straight or very shallow broad curve, builds anticipation |
| Exciting turn | 3–5s | visible stronger left/right sweep |
| Fast relief | 5–7s | **gentle continuous curve or rolling road**, not a dead-straight highway |
| Exciting S-turn | 3–5s | deliberately connected direction change; steer/countersteer meaningful |
| Danger corner | 2–3s | tight but readable; skilled braking or line choice useful |
| Recovery / downhill | 3–5s | easier sweeping descent, rewarding maintained speed |
| Repeat with variation | | alternate left/right, uphill/downhill, no copy-paste pattern |

Human's prior rhythm is a reference for *emotional cadence*, not an exact timer. The **easy segments may still curve**. After the opening acceleration section, target **no more than ~25–35% of driving distance with near-zero curvature**, and a significant navigable turn opportunity every ~5–10 seconds at typical fast driving. Tune based on recorded pacing, not arbitrary spline control-point count.

### Noticeable elevation

- Add at least **two clearly visible climbs/crests/descents in a 60–90s normal driving sequence**, one combined with a bend.
- The chase camera should clearly reveal approaching grade, road briefly disappearing behind a crest and a downhill panorama after the crest (without changing camera FOV/zoom based on speed).
- Prefer smooth, readable rolling hills with obvious height difference, not a jagged roller coaster. Surface normals, roadside props, asphalt raycast and collision/shoulder limits must stay in sync.
- Uphill/downhill transitions must not obscure an impossible sharp corner. Preserve road look-ahead enough for an arcade reaction window.

### Track continuity and difficulty

- v0.4.1 reportedly fixed a **mountain mesh masking the road**; do not reintroduce that or mesh gaps. Check updated hills/turns at high pace from multiple paths and view angles.
- Verify left/right S-curve plus crest can be driven **with the tuned analog controls** by steering/braking without falling into repeated unavoidable off-road penalties.
- Keep roadside object rhythm and lane-line optical flow producing the accepted speed feeling.
- Test at least **120 seconds simulated high-speed travel**, crossing track loop/segment boundaries; inspect actual rendered frames and surface ray hits for missing road/terrain intersections.

## Preservation / non-goals

- Keep `1.75×` world-pace default and any developer A/B selector; don't rescale actual movement to hide a handling issue.
- Keep car framing (~20–25% viewport width), non-speed-zoom chase camera and **working analog needle speedometer to the LEFT of the car**.
- Desktop: `A/D` steer, `Space` gas, `Enter` brake. Pixel 8: absolute-position horizontal steering strip + stacked gas/brake, hand swap, simultaneous touch, long-press suppression, 20:9 landscape fullscreen/PWA/safe areas. No extra on-screen buttons.
- Existing bounded shoulder, off-road speed penalty, player-only road recovery, mute/pause and sound logic remain. Gentle steering should not generate constant tire squeal.
- No new convertible/occupants, scene biome themes, traffic, opponents, score system, physics-engine swap or heavy architecture.
- Handoff with real playable v0.4.2, track/steering metrics, screenshots, motion clips, automated results and known limitations. Stop for human playtest.
