# v0.3 — Arcade Velocity

**Scope:** Visual framing and felt speed first; denser arcade corners next; roadside scenic themes are optional.
**Base:** Existing playable v0.2.1 hotfix on local OutRun branch (`9bdc6d8`); preserve code/handling/phone functionality.
**Source of inspiration:** User-provided OutRun 2 gameplay reference (~29 s), and v0.2.1 Pixel 8 screenshot compared to an OutRun 2 screenshot. These references are observational; never copy reference game assets.

## Product diagnosis (from human playtest)

1. The current vehicle is **too small and far away**: visually like driving a toy, with scenery/UI competing for attention. On screenshots normalized to equal **2048-pixel width**, current car is roughly **12%** of frame width; reference car roughly **25%**. Different aspect ratios (current ~20:9, reference ~16:9) make these approximate targets, not exact geometry.
2. The current car may display a high KM/H number but **feels like an old slow car**. Video reference builds from ~40 mph to ~180 mph over ~20–25 s and visibly accelerates roadside scenery/road markings past the player. We do **not** need to reproduce exact mph or acceleration numerics: make the in-world advance, optical flow and audio convey strong speed.
3. Previously requested: arcade-dense curves and sharper road challenges, not strictly realistic highway geometry. **This is P1 below**, after the two P0 items.
4. Distinct scenery every few dozen seconds is desirable but **BONUS (P2)**; do not sacrifice P0 quality for biome variety.

## P0-A — Chase framing: the vehicle AND nearby road dominate

**Goals**
- On Pixel 8-like landscape (20:9), car should occupy about **20–25% of viewport width** in steady chase view; desktop 16:9 should look comparably bold (**~20–26%**, allow rational aspect-responsive adjustment). These are starting visual targets, not reasons to clip the car or obscure upcoming curves.
- Position vehicle **lower in frame**: aim for car rear/bottom near **88–95% of viewport height** while keeping whole car visible; enough of the road ahead must be readable for difficult turns. Compare game screenshot and reference visually, not only by HUD/layout metrics.
- Improve camera height, pitch, fixed chase distance and fixed FOV as needed. **Do not create speed-responsive zoom/FOV/dolly** (v0.2 regression).
- Make road-to-car scale plausible: current car looks tiny against excessively broad pavement. Tune projected framing and, only if necessary, road/car world-scale ratio consistently so lane/shoulder collision and steering remain coherent. Avoid simply narrowing collision road or enlarging the car mesh independent of physics.
- Use car-forward composition: no massive empty expanse behind the car. Chase camera tracks vehicle even during drifts, not road center; look-ahead keeps upcoming bends visible. No disorienting shake.
- **Reduce UI competition**, especially on Pixel 8. Replace the oversized row of Pause/Restart/Sound/Settings/Exit fullscreen buttons with a minimal **Pause/Menu** affordance; expose the rest in an accessible paused overlay. Keep a compact speed display. Maintain comfortably tappable >=56 CSS px driving buttons, safe area, layout swap and easy fullscreen exit. Never sacrifice the two-thumb controls to enlarge the road.
- Avoid the reference game's SEGA brands, Ferrari shape/details, licensed graphics and proprietary art.

**Art constraint:** If the enlarged existing car exposes ugly placeholder construction, modestly refine silhouette, materials, lights and wheel detail as part of composition polish. No expensive new car asset pipeline.

## P0-B — Perceived velocity: speed must be visible and audible

At a consistent fixed camera composition, increasing actual car speed must visibly accelerate surrounding world motion, not merely display a changing number.

- Audit the relationship between **speed value, world distance traveled per second, road segment/stripe spacing, roadside prop spacing, camera tracking**, and what the viewer sees. If scene scale makes 200 km/h feel like 30, fix the world-unit/road-motion pipeline coherently.
- Add/re-tune near-road visual references (lane dashes, shoulder stripes, reflectors, posts, guardrails, foliage/cliffs/trees positioned closer to the road) that quickly pass the player and have clear near-versus-far **parallax**. Avoid repeating noisy textures causing aliasing at speed.
- Road should stream under the car convincingly; accelerating from low to high speed should yield an obvious rising flow rate in a **recorded video**, not a still image. Optimize draw calls/instancing/LOD and cap device pixel ratio as necessary.
- Improve engine note responsiveness/pitch and modest wind/tire feedback, safely user-gesture unlocked, without copying reference music/audio. Sound enhances real on-screen motion; it cannot replace it.
- Initial acceleration should feel eager; high speed should feel exhilarating **while still steerable and recoverable**. Balance real gameplay and visually dramatic forward progression. Do not solve by doubling HUD speed alone.
- No speed-based camera FOV, dolly/zoom, fake shaking, aggressive motion blur, constant particle spam or unreadable road. A subtle *fixed-composition* follow-camera lag is okay.
- Demonstrate a single uninterrupted acceleration-and-drive clip from launch through sustained high speed, plus matched low- and high-speed passes over a comparable roadside-marked segment.

## P1 — More challenging arcade-road rhythm

After P0 is convincingly demonstrated:

- Make bends **more frequent and stronger** than a realistic leisurely coast road: readable left/right transitions, an S-curve sequence, at least one tighter corner and one crest/bend combination during ~60–90 s normal driving.
- Arrange short breathers/straights between demanding sequences; avoid endless maximum-curvature turns, abrupt non-drivable spline kinks or blind punishment. The player must learn to **brake/setup/steer/drift** to preserve high average speed.
- A hands-off car at speed must still leave asphalt on meaningful bends. The v0.2.1 **bounded shoulder** must prevent open-world excursions, NOT automatically steer back to pavement. Keep off-road speed penalty and quick inward steering recovery.
- Geometry should have smooth 3D mesh, collision boundary and visuals in sync. Both bend directions and hill crest verified. Never raise difficulty merely by making keyboard/touch inputs twitchy or reducing traction to an uncontrollable state.

## P2 — BONUS: varied roadside vistas (non-blocking)

If P0 and P1 are well-tested and there is ample remaining budget, add **one** visually distinct themed transition after ~30–45 s at normal fast driving speed (e.g. coast → town, or coast → arid canyon), with coherent palette/props and no frame hitch. No multi-biome system or large handcrafted map in this milestone. Report **NOT IMPLEMENTED — BONUS** without calling the milestone incomplete if skipped.

## Performance and preservation

- Maintain original technology, existing responsive web/PWA, Chrome landscape/fullscreen, 20:9 Pixel 8 and desktop 16:9 support. No browser text selection on long steering/gas hold; no stuck pointers.
- At normal mobile landscape resolution aim sustained >=30 FPS on **actual Pixel 8**, preferably near 60, without knowingly degrading earlier performance. Desktop browser emulation cannot confirm device FPS/thermals.
- No new opponent AI/traffic, checkpoints, score/time modes, garage or public deployment.
- Implement targeted regression tests and actually inspect screenshots **and running motion**, guided by `docs/V0.3_ACCEPTANCE.md`.
