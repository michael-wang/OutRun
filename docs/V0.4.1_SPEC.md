# v0.4.1 — Analog Arcade Steering & Rescue

**State:** Specification revised BEFORE v0.4.1 implementation was handed to Codex.  
**Playable base:** local v0.4 (`04a1f2b` implementation / `ba76b22` handoff).  
**Human complaint:** v0.4 is sluggish entering a curve, then keeps drifting/yawing after steering ends; road sometimes visually disappears/ends; upper-right HUD is not the wanted left-of-car needle speedometer.  
**NEW steering design decision:** Stop trying to polish only the old binary button steering; introduce a continuous **virtual analog steering-position model** used by BOTH touch and keyboard.

## P0-A — One steering signal, two input methods

### Shared steering contract

The vehicle receives one desired steer value in the normalized continuous range **[-1.0, +1.0]**:
- -1.0: full left; -0.5: moderate left; near -0.15: slight left.
- 0: neutral wheel (no *new* yaw command).
- near +0.15: slight right; +0.5: moderate right; +1.0: full right.

This is a **steering-position spectrum**, not seven buttons and not a simulation of real steering-wheel revolutions. Show a visible movable thumb/cursor at its current target, center tick and obvious left/right zones. Consider a small dead zone around center (suggest ~5–8% of span) and a gentle response curve for accuracy. Tunings must be tested by play, not blindly accepted.

### Pixel 8 landscape: horizontal absolute-position steering strip

- Replace the two bottom-left steering buttons with **one horizontal steering control zone**, situated low on the designated steering-hand side. Starting layout: left thumb controls strip, right thumb holds stacked GAS over BRAKE; keep Settings swap (strip moves to right, pedals to left).
- Indicative layout: strip ~30–42% of usable landscape width, broad vertical hit area, comfortable within safe-area insets and reachable by one thumb. Adjust in actual 20:9 Pixel 8-like screenshots to avoid the car, road lookahead, analog speed gauge and system gesture/cutout areas. Do not place a full-width strip over the entire game.
- **Absolute input:** on `pointerdown`, compute normalized steering from the finger's horizontal position within the control zone. Touching center = 0; touching its left edge = -1; right edge = +1. **On `pointermove` with the captured pointer**, update continuously even when sliding a little outside the strip (clamp to ends).
- If the finger touches e.g. 25% across the rail, target approximately -0.5; 75% approximately +0.5. Do NOT interpret every touch as a binary left/right button or require dragging to first accumulate steering. An initial far-edge touch should request strong steering promptly.
- On `pointerup`, `pointercancel`, `lostpointercapture`, tab blur, pause or fullscreen transition, **steer target returns promptly to zero** to prevent stuck turning; do not preserve previous finger position.
- Only the pointer that acquired the steering strip drives that axis. A separate pointer ID can simultaneously hold GAS/BRAKE. Preserve brake vs gas precedence and correct per-pointer release ordering.
- Prevent long-press browser callouts/context menus, highlighting, scrolling and gesture capture on gameplay controls (e.g. scoped `touch-action:none` and `user-select:none`, pointer capture/cancel). Do not globally break ordinary Settings interaction.
- Gesture input is continuous but easily understood; visual marker/center should match real effective steering. Avoid introducing noticeable touch latency.

### PC: keyboard moves the SAME virtual steering position

- **A / D** are primary (left/right arrow aliases optional): holding A moves the virtual position toward -1, D toward +1 at a defined frame-time-independent travel rate, clamped at full lock. When **neither steering key is held**, the virtual position returns toward **0** with a fast spring-to-center behavior; while one key remains held it continues to control direction. Opposite simultaneous keys resolve deterministically.
- An example calibration starting point: from center to full lock in **~0.35–0.55s** with a held key, and neutral return in **~0.10–0.20s** after release. These are *starting guesses*, not realism requirements. Crucially, the **first 100–150ms of keypress must already have meaningful steering**, unlike v0.4's late ramp.
- The virtual position is not reset to zero every animation frame while a key remains down. Animate a visible pointer on the same conceptual horizontal strip (a small PC display optional) for debugging/teaching.
- Space = GAS (hold); Enter = BRAKE (hold). Manage gameplay event focus: suppress Space page scroll and Enter activation of focused game menu only while racing. Do not break accessibility in menus/settings. Keyup, blur, pause clear input.
- PC and mobile must go through the same shared `steeringTarget` and vehicle driving model; don't build separate steering physics per platform.

## P0-B — Direct arcade kinematics, NOT a car simulator

- Diagnose/diff v0.3.2 → v0.4 turning pipeline (target ramp, yaw-rate inertia, velocity heading vs car body heading, drift offset). Document why tap is weak and release leaves unwanted yaw/slide. Do not simply revert to the twitchy v0.3.2 mapping.
- Treat requested normalized steering as **driver intent**, converted promptly to a desired turning response. Minimal input/steer latency (one/few frames), **no long integration of yaw inertia**. Release toward 0 should extinguish continued commanded yaw quickly; reversal should work promptly.
- At neutral, retain the car's current world heading and forward motion (rather than magically pointing along the road). There may be tiny decaying slip, but no sideways drift across multiple lanes because of stale control states.
- Do not automatically steer onto spline tangent, reposition toward asphalt, solve bends, recenter a lane, or reduce accepted speed **1.75×** to hide a bug.
- Player must still actually drive through a bend; no-steer at speed eventually exits asphalt and suffers existing speed penalty. The v0.2.1 bounded shoulder keeps the car close to the road, with inward-steer recovery, without acting like lane assist.
- Full wheel lock must make a tight turn possible at a reasonable speed, and small fraction values should permit accurate high-speed line corrections. Avoid abrupt uncorrectable instant rotation and full-circle spins. If speed-dependent max yaw is used, tune with real browser driving at 1.75×.
- Preserve expressive *rendered* drift attitude as a restrained effect of substantial actual slip. Don't introduce a new simulated tire model to reproduce old uncontrolled inertia. Gentle steering should NOT routinely squeal; squeal starts only for meaningful slide, with hysteresis.

**Core principle: press → respond, release → stop adding yaw, reverse → promptly respond.**
Wheel self-centering ≠ car road-centering.

## P0-C — Track continuity regression

The player reports some road sections appear to end/disconnect after v0.4. This is a P0 defect to diagnose, not a visual flourish.

- Inspect spline/beat-map construction, segment seams and cyclic wrap, 3D mesh generation, terrain joins, culling, road width/height/normals, camera lookahead and recycling/streaming. Reproduce with the new 1.75× effective pace over multiple loops if possible.
- Run at least **120 simulated seconds** of high-speed driving across all region/loop seams, including 30/60 FPS equivalence, and inspect real browser frames at and ahead of vehicle. Use actual mesh/raycast checks to validate continuous driveable asphalt rather than only track progress numbers.
- If the discontinuity is found, fix its root cause (not a disguised teleport/camera jump). If not reproducible, document investigation and status **UNVERIFIED, NOT FIXED**; do not claim a successful complete road repair.
- It is acceptable to simplify a broken overly-complex bend from v0.4's new road rhythm to restore continuity. Do not spend time adding more demanding turns.

## P0-D — Needle speedometer, LEFT of car

- Replace current v0.4 upper-right numeric card with original **circular analog dial + working needle**, clear ticks/scale, upper-speed accent and digital km/h.
- The dial should be **to the player's car left side in screen space**, with the rear of the car still strongly framed (~20–25% landscape width). Position low/left like the user's original OutRun 2 reference rather than top-right, but not underneath the Pixel 8 left steering rail.
- At desktop 1280×720, implement a tasteful readable gauge, not just a rotated needle placed over a decorative number. At Pixel 8-like 20:9 and narrow 844×390, adapt gauge size/vertical placement above steering controls and avoid overlap in **both** swapped-hand configurations (right-strip/left-pedals included).
- The gauge is noninteractive (e.g. `pointer-events:none`), must never eat driving touches. Fixed overlay position; no swaying with the car. Real game indicated speed drives the digital number and needle consistently.
- Test stationary, medium and high values, visible continuous needle motion, no unwanted HUD jitter, high contrast, full-screen cutout/safe-area handling.
- Do not copy Ferrari/SEGA dial art or logos.

## Explicit non-goals and integration

- No new tracks, biomes, traffic, convertible or passenger animation. Don't expand v0.4's drift physics while fixing controls. Preserve original Three.js + TypeScript + Vite stack.
- Preserve accepted 1.75× high-speed world motion, fixed camera FOV/dolly (no speed zoom), PC control mapping, Pixel 8 landscape/fullscreen/PWA, independent gas/brake touch, left/right swap, long-press protection, off-road speed penalty/outer boundary, muted audio, game menu.
- Implement shared analog controller first, then road continuity, then analog gauge. Run browser at both desktop and simulated phone viewports; test **on real Pixel 8 when accessible**, otherwise mark device behavior UNVERIFIED.
- Create `docs/V0.4.1_HANDOFF.md` with code diff/root causes, analog input mapping + replay/video, track loop verification, phone+desktop screenshots, build/unit/E2E results and known issues. No push/merge/deploy. Stop for human test.
