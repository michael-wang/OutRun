### P0-A. GEAR-SHIFT SPEED DROOP — dynamic, audible, visually legible

- First investigate measured real current v0.8.1 **time-series**, NOT just code constants. Record `speedKmh`, `worldDistance`, `gear`, `rpm`, `wheelDriveForce` / shiftTorqueFactor and `netLongitudinalAcceleration` at 30/60fps across all five upshifts. Compare baseline to new; 0→100/200/300 and gear-by-gear average a. Video reference may be visually consulted if locally available; don't invent its measured shifts.
- Genuine shift should contain 1) power/torque release, 2) engine rpm synchronization/drop, 3) torque restoration with modest controlled rebound. Tune short state/interval ~0.2–0.45s first, possibly more in lower/early shifts for arcade feel. **Actual vehicle velocity is integrated from longitudinal net force; numeric and needle gauges display this same velocity.** NO artificial subtraction by e.g. `speedKmh -= 5`. RPM dropping ≠ wheel speed dropping.
- To make small speed dips **visibly perceptible** especially at upper-gear shifts, real net deceleration must overcome drive torque: aerodynamic drag + rolling/coasting + plausible brief shift-related driveline/inertial torque drag. A 1–3 km/h temporary dip in some upper shifts is an initial feel target, NOT an absolute hardcoded deduction or universal simulation truth. Early shifts may primarily pause acceleration. Do not make car jerk backward or make every shift subtract the same number.
- Preserve nonlinear stronger low gear / weaker high gear acceleration from v0.8.1; cap actual longitudinal deceleration appropriately so six-speed/300 remains pleasant. Preserve warm approved engine sound and gear-rpm coupling, no new shrill note / repeat shift hunting. Measure old/new speed-vs-time and natural dips by 5 shifts, draw simple plot/table. If data shows no natural actual drop at low gears despite adequate torque interruption, report plainly rather than fake.

### P0-B. FLAT 300 equilibrium; grade-dependent higher/lower attainable speed

- **300 km/h is NOMINAL flat-road top**, NOT an unconditional `clamp(v,0,300)`. On a sufficiently long level full-gas road, engine power / gearing / quadratic aerodynamic drag settle near 300. A genuine sustained descent can increase actual vehicle speed past 300 if downhill gravity/engine/backdrive dominates rolling+drag; sustained uphill with full gas at 300 should lose speed until a lower equilibrium, no stuck full-throttle 300 plateau.
- Inspect original grade-term from v0.7D.1 and v0.8.1 torque/drag to ensure **same slope from actual `height(s)`** and avoid scaling 1.75× travel a second time. No fake altitude-specific top-speed cutoff or unconditional downward/upward HUD offset. `dv/dt` depends on signed grade, speed² drag and wheel drive, not current biome label.
- Initial user-visible test target: a sustained representative real downhill may reach **315–330 km/h**, without instant burst; steep uphill at 300 drops clearly after several seconds. These are tuning/examples, not mandates if actual graded track length/physics differs.
- Protect technical safety from runaway speed with a **high defensive ceiling** (e.g. ~350–360 indicated km/h) only if needed for geometry or renderer stability. Default gauge, gear display, visual speed and physics must not truncate at 300; extend dial scale at least to maximum demonstrated overrun (e.g. 350/360). At actual >300 maintain stable lateral pressure / steering and skid audio; 6th gear engine RPM limiter/coasting behavior should make sense, not spin endlessly past redline or force engine to accelerate forever.
- Measured benchmark at same initial state: level vs uphill vs downhill with `throttle=1` and `throttle=0`. Include an actual hill crest from Stage One with smooth grade continuity and no instant speed discontinuity, plus high-FPS stability and display.
