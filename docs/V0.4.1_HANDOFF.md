## Changes and diagnosis

The old v0.4 pipeline combined an 850ms nonlinear hold ramp, exponential steering smoothing, delayed drift exit and low velocity-heading grip. Short presses barely reached authority; release could retain steering and substantial sideways velocity. The new car consumes the shared analog target directly, with no second steering ramp. Keyboard reaches full lock in 400ms and returns in at most 125ms; reversal unloads existing lock at the return rate before building the opposite lock. Touch is immediate, with a small center dead zone and endpoint detents. Positions 10/25/50/75/90% measured approximately -0.79/-0.48/0/+0.48/+0.79.

In matched 150ms keyboard presses at initial 234 km/h, world yaw changes 1.93° versus 0.81° before; lateral movement is 0.138m. Left/right results mirror. This strengthens initial response without returning to instantaneous binary full lock. Analog release commands zero yaw immediately; keyboard release finishes returning within 125ms.

Zero target produces zero commanded yaw. Slip damps toward the **current body heading**, never the road tangent. Substantial full-lock turns retain real slip, bounded shoulders and inward-steer recovery. Default coherent world pace stays 1.75; camera FOV and dolly remain fixed. A badly chosen heading can still carry the car off the asphalt after release, as required.

The road obstruction was reproduced: v0.4 extended the route beyond the old distant mountain ring, whose center (400,500) and inner radius 1550m were fixed for the previous route. Its opaque double-sided surface crossed asphalt between sampled distances 3085–4350m. At distance 3195m, lateral +5m, the ridge was 20.681m above the road. A 5m-spaced scan across three lateral lanes found 202 obstructed samples. This was scenery covering a continuous road, not a missing spline segment. The existing mountain ring now encloses the actual route bounds plus 400m clearance. The same 3,756-sample scan finds zero obstructions.
