# Proposed v0.7C.1 — Six-Speed 300 km/h Powertrain & Softer Engine Sound

**Status:** owner-requested vehicle-feel changes, specified for Codex; NOT yet implemented. **Do not interpret this as an L2-composition fix**. The owner also supplied a ~40-second racing reference video for L1 event-count analysis; accurate media inspection is REQUIRED before anyone stipulates a new L2 event-count target. Do not invent event counts without watching the actual uploaded source.

## User requests

1. Current acceleration reaches top speed too quickly; **gear shifting must become an enjoyable part of driving**. Implement **six forward gears**.
2. Existing top speed ~238 km/h is too low; make the player's original convertible capable of **300 km/h displayed and physically in the coherent game travel model**.
3. Existing synthesized engine sound is **too sharp/shrill and unpleasant**; make it lower, fuller, smoother, and comfortable during a prolonged driving session, while keeping recognizable high-RPM energy and satisfying shift events.

**Non-goal:** full simulation drivetrain; do not trade away the human-approved fast arcade lateral steering / drift model merely to model gear ratios.

## P0 drivetrain behavior: six recognizable gears

- Choose **six-speed automatic shifting for this first tune** (no new gear buttons, no changes to existing Pixel 8 two-thumb controls or PC A/D+Space/Enter). Later manual shifting may be evaluated as a separate option only after the automatic experience feels good. The word “six gears” must mean actual distinct gear state, RPM and shift behavior, not a cosmetic HUD animation.
- Distinct forward gears `1,2,3,4,5,6` with appropriate ratio/speed envelope and smooth hysteresis on upshift/downshift. At full acceleration the player should encounter FIVE upshift events before nearing the new top speed, accompanied by brief RPM fall, modest audible powertrain unload/reengage and a slight short torque transition; **not** jerky car path motion.
- Use a coherent engine-speed / wheel-speed / gear relationship in an **arcade model**, with idle RPM, upshift threshold, downshift threshold and rev range explicitly configurable. Engine sound driven predominantly by normalized RPM/throttle/load, not just vehicle displayed km/h; decelerating and re-accelerating within a gear sound different.
- Do not gear-hunt under small speed oscillations or off-road slowdown; braking causes believable gradual downshift transitions. At zero speed engine idles, no nonzero fictitious wheel speed.
- Display a small readable current gear next to the established analog speedometer without obscuring road or mobile controls. Tiny engine RPM bar/needle is OPTIONAL, not an excuse for a HUD redesign.

### Acceleration pacing

Measure v0.7C's CURRENT full-throttle baseline first: `0→100`, `0→200`, `0→238` and shift behavior (none/previous simulation). Then retune.

**Starting HUMAN-PLAYTEST target envelopes** from stop on road at full gas:
- `0→100 km/h` about **4–6 seconds**: still feels punchy.
- `0→200 km/h` about **14–20 seconds** total: more time to savor acceleration.
- `0→300 km/h` about **32–45 seconds** total: top speed feels earned rather than instantly reached.

These are initial proposals, not measurements or fixed realistic car specifications. Codex may adjust within reason based on visual feel, but MUST report actual bench results. Faster/slower changes should not be silently masked by altering the accepted **1.75× world pace**.

- `300 km/h` must be attainable under normal held throttle on a flat sufficiently LONG uninterrupted in-game road, not only a test override. Respect the game's current consistent indicated-speed → longitudinal/world progression mapping. Digital + analog speed readouts agree, and scale/tick marks remain legible at 300.
- Maintain the original road-relative Arcade Lateral Balance corner pressure rules at the established reference speed around 230 km/h. A curve that was full-gas traversable at 230 may demand more correction or braking at 300; that is an understandable consequence of increased speed. Do not secretly boost player grip or reduce curvature force to make all existing bends flat-out at 300.
- Keep bounded off-road speed penalty, braking authority and no-lane-centering. No artificial RPM/gear changes should alter world pose or add yaw inertia.

## P0 engine audio: reduce harshness without eliminating personality

The previous synthetic engine is reported as **uncomfortably sharp**, especially after repeated listening. Fix the actual timbre, not just reduce master volume.

- Inspect actual Web Audio graph, waveforms/synths/filter envelopes/sample rate and high-speed pitch scaling. Identify whether excessively bright oscillator harmonics, high fundamental frequencies, unfiltered noise or clipping/compression drive harshness.
- Prefer a **warmer lower/midrange mechanical/exhaust texture**, moderate harmonics and controlled higher-frequency energy. Smooth frequency/envelope changes across throttle/RPM and through gear shifts; avoid piercing pure tones, sharp sawtooth aliasing, pops, unexpected gain spikes and exhausting sustained high-speed screech.
- A six-speed shift must be AUDIBLE: distinct rising RPM note, brief pitch drop/quiet torque break, and rebuild in next gear. Engine and tire squeal remain distinct, and skid sounds don't play loudly on harmless light steering.
- Use existing Web Audio and original procedural sound where possible. No unlicensed engine recordings, streaming assets or new audio package unless a demonstrable need exists.
- Preserve user-gesture-required audio unlock, mute/pause and desktop/mobile compatibility; do not auto-start sound before an eligible gesture.
- Capture before/after audio for actual human listening. Automated checks can confirm no clipping, smooth envelopes and RPM response but **cannot certify subjective comfort**.
